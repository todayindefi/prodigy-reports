What Went Well

  • The dialogue explainer was the clear reach winner. “Why not just take the highest rate?” earned 1,116 impressions, more than twice the next post, while turning linked-price risk into a simple conversation.
  • Concrete HYPE choices generated the strongest intent. The $80-at-58% versus $78-at-43% comparison earned 34 engagements, 4 bookmarks and 2 clicks from 545 impressions.
  • Risk transparency continued to perform. Posts explaining conversion, expiry commitment and the asset received at settlement reached engagement rates as high as 6.82%.
  • All 10 original posts cleared 350 impressions. The core publishing cadence remained consistent even after the launch spike normalized.

Areas for Improvement

  • Total impressions fell 46% to 5,274 after the HyperEVM launch week. Some normalization was expected, but average impressions also declined 31% to 203.
  • URL clicks fell from 12 to 6. Educational content generated attention, but most posts did not give the reader a specific next action.
  • Replies remain inefficient. Sixteen reply tweets produced only 360 impressions, and 9 of them generated zero engagement.
  • The top-reach post had only a 2.06% engagement rate and zero clicks. Its format distributed well but did not carry readers toward a vault decision.
  • Several posts repeated the same fixed-rate and no-early-exit concepts. Repetition needs new scenarios, visuals or comparison points to remain useful.

Recommendations for Next Week

  • Build a recurring linked-price comparison series. Start with the live asset price, show two linked prices and yields, then explain how the settlement outcomes differ.
  • Keep the dialogue format in weekly rotation. Use it to answer one genuine objection at a time, then follow with a concrete vault example.
  • Reduce affirmation-only replies. Reply when the account can add a number, clarification, example or useful question.
  • Add a specific next step to high-reach education. Ask readers to compare two live levels or inspect a named expiry instead of ending with a generic link.
  • Use one visual settlement table each week. It can make recurring mechanics bookmarkable without repeating the same copy.
