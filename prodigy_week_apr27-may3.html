Areas for Improvement

  • Average engagement rate dropped 44% (17.2% → 9.6%). The V2 launch tweet had only 1.39% ER despite massive reach — a classic broadcast-style trade-off. When a tweet reaches far beyond the core audience, ER naturally compresses. This isn’t a content quality issue, but it’s worth noting.
  • Reply tweets got minimal traction. The 7 reply tweets (ranging from 1 to 46 impressions) contributed almost nothing to engagement totals. Launch-day replies like follow-up context tweets were buried by the algorithm. Replies should be reserved for high-value conversations, not used as content distribution.
  • 8 tweets on Apr 29 diluted per-tweet reach. Launch day saw 8 tweets published (the announcement + follow-ups + replies). While understandable for a product launch, the volume likely caused algorithmic suppression on the lower-performing posts. The “more details” tweet got only 281 impressions and 3.2% ER despite being tied to the launch.
  • Replies decreased 13% (39 → 34). Despite a massive reach increase, actual conversation (replies from others) went down. The V2 content was more broadcast-oriented and less conversation-starting than last week’s humor and relatable content.
  • Non-V2 tweets didn’t benefit from the launch halo. Tweets on May 1–3 (post-launch) averaged just 177 impressions — barely above last week’s 162 average. The V2 launch didn’t create a lasting lift in organic reach for subsequent tweets.

Recommendations for Next Week
